# Synthesis 013 — competing skills and the reconciliation edge

## What Source 015 changes

The three-target study supplies primary-repository examples for bounded gates, artifact-addressed
continuity, fresh task execution, independent review, and the drift risk of prompt-maintained status.
It turns two previously distributed implications into explicit concepts:

1. **PZL-172:** lifecycle promotion needs a minimum machine-checkable evidence reconciliation
   envelope whose closed properties are deterministic and whose semantic uncertainty escalates.
2. **PZL-173:** a run-local recovery ledger is useful only while subordinate to canonical Mission
   intent and evidence.

## What it reinforces

- The host runtime owns workers, context isolation, worktrees, edits, tests, commits, and model
  selection; Spectacular owns the portable contract and lifecycle interpretation.
- Review-repair needs bounded attempts and escalation, not reviewer satisfaction as a terminal
  condition.
- Status and briefings should be derived projections, not independently maintained prose or fixed
  percentages.
- Artifact richness can improve resumption while simultaneously creating taxonomy and routing
  burden; each artifact must earn a unique query or transition.

## What it does not justify

- No target proves that Spectacular should become an autonomous execution framework.
- No target proves that a separate implementation-runner companion has enough standalone value.
- No target proves the minimum reconciliation schema or its user value.
- No accepted S01-S03 contract is reversed, and the twelve-session order remains stable.

## Decision-program impact

PZL-172 and PZL-173 join S06. Their prerequisites remain S03 truth/freshness and S05 authority;
therefore the suggested disposable validator is **not authorized yet**. After those contracts settle,
S06 should define the envelope and may request the smallest fixture-based spike that could falsify
it. S07 should treat the implementation-runner as a needs-evidence candidate, not a default
companion.
